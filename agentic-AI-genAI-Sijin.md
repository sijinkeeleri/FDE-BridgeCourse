The **"Brain in a Jar"** analogy describes a standard **Generative AI model** as having intelligence but no ability to interact with the outside world. It can only generate responses based on the knowledge it learned during training and the information provided in the current prompt. It cannot independently browse the internet, access databases, use external tools, or retrieve the latest information. As a result, its responses may be outdated or incomplete when asked about recent events.

An **Agentic AI** system overcomes this limitation by combining the language model with external tools, planning, and the ability to perform actions. Instead of relying solely on its pre-trained knowledge, it can decide what steps are required, use search tools or APIs to gather current information, process the results, and then generate a final response.

### Example: Summarizing the latest five research papers on climate change

* **Standard Generative AI:**

  * Can summarize climate change research only if the papers are included in the prompt or were part of its training data.
  * Cannot independently find the latest research papers published after its knowledge cutoff.
  * May provide outdated or incomplete information.

* **Agentic AI:**

  1. Searches trusted research sources (such as academic databases or search engines) for the latest climate change papers.
  2. Retrieves the five most recent and relevant papers.
  3. Reads and extracts the key findings from each paper.
  4. Generates a concise summary highlighting the main contributions, methods, and conclusions.
  5. Presents the results with references or links to the original papers.

In short, a standard Generative AI is limited to generating text from existing knowledge, whereas an Agentic AI can **plan, gather up-to-date information, use external tools, and complete multi-step tasks autonomously**, making it far more effective for real-world applications requiring current data.
