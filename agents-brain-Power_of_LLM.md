**"Stochastic Parrots"**

The term **stochastic parrot** suggests that a Large Language Model (LLM) predicts the next word based on patterns learned from massive amounts of training data. It does not truly understand concepts or think like a human. Instead, it generates responses by recognizing statistical relationships between words and phrases. This view emphasizes that LLMs are excellent at producing fluent text but are limited by their training data.

**"General-Purpose Reasoning Engines"**

The term **general-purpose reasoning engine** views an LLM as more than a text generator. It can analyze information, solve problems, plan tasks, follow logical steps, and adapt its reasoning to different domains. While it still predicts tokens, its ability to perform reasoning, decision-making, and planning makes it useful for solving complex tasks.

### Which view is more useful for building an AI agent?

For building an **AI agent**, the **general-purpose reasoning engine** perspective is more useful.

An AI agent must:

* Understand user goals.
* Break complex tasks into smaller steps.
* Decide which tools or APIs to use.
* Evaluate intermediate results.
* Adapt its actions based on new information.
* Produce a final, accurate response.

For example, if asked to **summarize the latest five research papers on climate change**, an AI agent would:

1. Plan the required steps.
2. Search academic databases or trusted sources.
3. Retrieve the latest papers.
4. Read and analyze each paper.
5. Summarize the key findings.
6. Present the results with references.

This requires reasoning, planning, and tool usage rather than simply generating text.

### Conclusion

While the **stochastic parrot** view explains how LLMs generate language at a technical level, the **general-purpose reasoning engine** perspective is more practical for designing AI agents. It highlights the LLM's ability to reason, plan, and coordinate with external tools, enabling autonomous completion of complex, real-world tasks.
